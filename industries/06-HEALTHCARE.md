## PALETTE GUIDANCE

Healthcare sites must feel safe, trustworthy, and hopeful — never cold or clinical.

**Avoid:**
- Pure clinical white + cold blue (reads as hospital, not welcoming clinic)
- Bright red (reads as emergency/danger)
- High-contrast neon (reads as aggressive, wrong for trust)

**Lean toward:**
- Warm neutrals: cream, off-white, warm sand, soft linen backgrounds
- Muted anchor colors: deep teal, sage green, dusty blue, warm terracotta, espresso, slate
- One warm accent: gold, soft amber, dusty rose, or warm coral — used sparingly
- Dark sections: deep espresso, charcoal, or deep teal (never pure black)

**For addiction recovery / mental health specifically:**
- Lead with calm, empathy, and hope
- Muted, desaturated palettes — nothing energetic or loud
- Soft greens, muted blues, warm neutrals
- Avoid anything that reads as "party" or "nightlife"

**For chiropractic / physical therapy:**
- Can be slightly bolder and more energetic than mental health
- Warm teal, deep navy, or sage work well as primaries
- Gold or amber as accent for warmth and credibility

## TYPOGRAPHY GUIDANCE

**Recommended archetypes:**

**Warm Authority** (best for most healthcare):
- Display: Elegant serif (Playfair Display, Cormorant Garamond, Lora)
- Body: Clean humanist sans (Source Sans 3, Figtree, DM Sans)
- Feel: Trusted, established, warm, professional

**Premium Professional** (modern clinics, sports chiropractic, performance health):
- Display: Humanist or geometric display sans (DM Sans ExtraBold, Satoshi Black, Plus Jakarta Sans)
- Body: Figtree, DM Sans, Nunito Sans
- Feel: Modern, confident, premium

**Never use:**
- Heavy grotesque / condensed display (reads industrial, wrong for healthcare) (exception: sports/performance-positioned chiropractic and athletic-rehab brands per Master Playbook Rule 11)
- Decorative or script fonts for body or UI text

## COPY TONE

Healthcare copy must lead with the patient's pain and end with the transformation.

**Do:**
- Lead with empathy: "Living with chronic pain changes everything."
- State the outcome: "We help you get back to the life you love."
- Be specific about what you treat and how
- Use warm, approachable language — not clinical jargon
- First visit sections should reduce anxiety, not add to it

**Don't:**
- Lead with the clinic's history or awards
- Use overly clinical or technical language in the hero
- Use vague language: "holistic care" without explaining what that means
- Use fear-based copy ("if you don't treat this now...")

**For addiction recovery / mental health:**
- Always lead with empathy and destigmatization
- Crisis resources must be visible (SAMHSA hotline: 1-800-662-4357 / Crisis line: 988)
- Never use language that shames or stigmatizes the condition
- Copy must make the reader feel seen and safe before it asks them to act

## HEALTHCARE-SPECIFIC TRUST SIGNALS

Priority order:

1. **Google reviews with real Google G logo** — star rating + review count
2. **Years in practice** — e.g. "20+ years of experience"
3. **Patients helped** — e.g. "5,000+ patients cared for"
4. **Satisfaction / recommendation rate** — e.g. "98% would recommend"
   *(Use the practice's REAL numbers from the brief. The figures above are format
   examples only — never ship an invented statistic as if it were verified.)*
5. **Insurance accepted** — reduces a primary objection immediately
6. **Named doctors with credentials** — real people, real photos, real training
7. **Certifications** — Webster Technique, board certifications, specialty training
8. **Verbatim patient testimonials** — real names, real outcomes

## COMMON PATIENT OBJECTIONS (Address in design)

| Objection | Where to address |
|---|---|
| "Does my insurance cover this?" | Hero area, sidebar, FAQ, near form |
| "Is this safe / will it hurt?" | Services detail, FAQ, first visit section |
| "Are these doctors qualified?" | About page, credentials section, sidebar |
| "How many visits will I need?" | FAQ, first visit section |
| "Do you treat [my specific condition]?" | What We Treat page, home condition pills |
| "What happens at my first visit?" | About page, FAQ |
| "Is this covered for children / pregnancy?" | FAQ, pediatric / prenatal service pages |

## HOMEPAGE SECTION ORDER (Healthcare)

1. Announcement bar (insurances accepted + phone)
2. Hero (condition/outcome-led headline, dual CTA, trust micro-element)
3. Trust/Stats bar (patients helped, years, rating, recommendation rate)
4. Brand story / Welcome panel
5. Services preview grid (photo-forward, 3 cards)
6. What We Treat (condition pills)
7. Testimonials (real Google reviews)
8. Locations (map + cards)
9. Why Choose Us (differentiators)
10. Meet the Doctors
11. CTA conversion band
12. FAQ + Free consult form (PageOutro)
13. Footer

## COMPLIANCE REQUIREMENTS

- **HIPAA notice** below every patient contact form
- Privacy Policy linked in footer
- If telehealth is offered: note state licensing
- For addiction/recovery: crisis resource links visible (988 Lifeline, SAMHSA)
- For mental health: crisis resources in footer or persistent element

---

## PHOTOGRAPHY DIRECTION

- Warm, human, natural light — not clinical or cold
- Real clinic photos preferred over stock (waiting room, treatment areas, team)
- Patients in natural healing settings — not sterile, not posed awkwardly
- Doctor shots: approachable, confident, candid — not stiff headshots
- For mental health: calm environments, soft light, nature, human connection
- Consistent editing style across all images (warm temperature, moderate contrast)
- Never: fake smiling patients, cliché handshake shots, generic medical stock
