# Design Scarlet Macaw — General Industry Playbook
### Typical fits: any business not covered by the Healthcare, Legal, Real Estate, or
### Industrial playbooks. If a business only partly fits one of those, use this file and
### borrow the specific parts of that playbook that apply.
### Examples: Restaurants, fitness studios, beauty salons, spas, automotive, pet services, education, events, photography, coaching, agencies, retail, and any other service or product business.


## HOW TO READ THIS PLAYBOOK

**This file narrows the search space. It does not choose the design.**

1. **Client brand assets always win.** If the brief supplies real brand colors, fonts,
   or a logo, those are the anchor — build around them, never instead of them.
   Everything below is the fallback for a business with no established brand.
2. **Constraints bind; recommendations don't.** "Never / Avoid" items are real — they
   exist because those choices reliably misfire in this vertical. Palette leans, type
   archetypes, component lists, and section orders are **starting points and menus**:
   take what this specific business justifies, leave the rest.
3. **Never ship a whole list.** Every list here is a menu, not a checklist. Taking all
   of it is precisely how ten different businesses end up with the same website.
4. **Trends move; this file will lag them.** Where a current, better-executed pattern
   serves the same goal, use it and note the swap in one line in the Compliance Report.
   The target is a site that looks made in the year it shipped — not one that matches
   this document.

## TIER 2 COMPONENTS — DERIVE FROM THE BRIEF

There is no fixed Tier 2 set for the General playbook. The Synthesizer and Claude Design derive which additional components are needed based on the brief:

- **Named team members?** → Unlock Meet the Team (H10, A5) if the business features specific people (coaches, stylists, instructors, etc.)
- **Physical locations?** → Unlock Locations page and location cards
- **Portfolio / past work?** → Unlock a Portfolio or Gallery section (relevant for photographers, designers, event planners, renovation)
- **Booking / reservations?** → Emphasize booking CTA and process steps prominently
- **Menu / pricing?** → Add a Pricing or Menu section if the brief calls for it
- **Client intake forms?** → Unlock Forms page if needed
- **Certifications / credentials?** → Unlock Certifications section if the business has relevant qualifications

**Default rule:** if it's in the brief, it gets a section. If it's not in the brief, it
doesn't. Unlocking a component is permission, not an instruction — a thin section is
worse than an absent one.

## SUB-ARCHETYPE DECISION TREE
*(Use this table to pick a starting direction, then still adapt from the brief per the
sections below — this doesn't replace the brief-driven process, it accelerates the
first pass.)*

| Sub-archetype | Signal in the brief | Starting palette lean | Starting typography lean | Nav/CTA emphasis | Common Tier 2 unlocks |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **SaaS / software** | "platform," "dashboard," "subscription," per-seat or tiered pricing | Bold Modern or Premium Professional; frequently a single saturated brand-accent on neutral/dark | Bold Modern or Premium Professional | "Start free trial" / "Book a demo" as primary CTA, pricing prominent in nav | Pricing tiers, feature comparison table, integrations/logos bar |
| **E-commerce / product** | products for direct sale, cart/checkout mentioned, SKUs or a product catalog | Fun & Playful or Premium & Sophisticated depending on price point | Premium Professional (higher-end) or Bold Modern (younger brand) | "Shop Now" primary CTA, cart icon in nav, category-driven nav structure | Product grid, category pages, reviews-per-product, size/variant selectors |
| **Nonprofit / cause-driven** | donations, volunteers, mission statement, impact metrics | Trustworthy & Established or Warm & Approachable; avoid anything that reads corporate-cold | Warm Authority | "Donate" as primary CTA (distinct color from secondary CTAs), "Volunteer" as secondary | Impact stats/counters, program pages, donor recognition, volunteer sign-up form |
| **Education / courses** | curriculum, enrollment, instructors, cohorts or self-paced | Warm Authority palette lean, Calm & Reassuring | Warm Authority or Premium Professional | "Enroll Now" / "Apply" primary CTA, program list in nav | Curriculum/syllabus section, instructor bios, testimonials from alumni, FAQ (heavy — admissions questions) |
| **Hospitality (non-healthcare)** — hotels, venues, events | booking dates, guest capacity, amenities, event types | Premium & Sophisticated | Warm Authority | "Book Now" / "Check Availability" primary CTA, dates/calendar prominent | Gallery, amenities grid, room/venue types, reviews |
| **Professional services** — consulting, agencies, financial/accounting (non-legal) | retainer/engagement language, case studies, client roster | Trustworthy & Established or Premium & Sophisticated | Premium Professional | "Schedule a Consultation" primary CTA | Case studies/portfolio, process steps, team credentials, client logos bar |

If a brief doesn't clearly match one row, fall back to the brief-driven derivation process
above — don't force a business into the nearest archetype if it genuinely doesn't fit.

## PALETTE GUIDANCE

*Starting directions, not a prescription. If the client has brand colors, those win.
The 3 build directions must not all sit in the same corner of this space (Rule 1).*

Without industry-specific palette conventions, derive the palette from the brief's tone, audience, and reference sites.

**General palette principles:**

- Maximum 3 brand colors (primary, secondary, accent) + neutral palette
- Neutral palette: off-white, warm white, or soft gray for backgrounds — never pure white only
- Dark sections: deep charcoal, navy, espresso, or dark version of the primary — never pure black (#000000)
- Section alternation: light → soft neutral → dark → light — never all one tone
- Accent color: used sparingly to pull attention to CTAs and key moments

**Palette by tone (derive from brief Section 3):**

| Tone | Palette direction |
|---|---|
| Warm & approachable | Warm neutrals, earth tones, soft coral or amber accent |
| Premium & sophisticated | Deep anchor color, gold or champagne accent, cream backgrounds |
| Bold & energetic | High contrast, vivid accent, strong dark sections |
| Calm & reassuring | Muted, desaturated tones, soft greens or blues, gentle contrast |
| Trustworthy & established | Navy or charcoal anchor, gold or slate accent, warm white |
| Fun & playful | Brighter palette acceptable, but still restrained — one vivid accent max |

**Always avoid:** pure red as a primary (reads as danger/urgency unless trade or food service), pure black body text (use #0D0D0D or #1A1A1A), more than 3 brand colors.

## TYPOGRAPHY GUIDANCE

**Recommended archetypes:**

**Premium Professional** (most service businesses — default choice):
- Display: DM Sans ExtraBold, Satoshi Black, Plus Jakarta Sans ExtraBold
- Body: Instrument Sans, DM Sans, Nunito Sans
- Feel: Modern, confident, trustworthy

**Warm Authority** (wellness, coaching, education, community-focused):
- Display: Playfair Display Bold, Lora Bold, Cormorant Garamond SemiBold
- Body: Source Sans 3, Figtree
- Feel: Established, warm, credible

**Bold Modern** (fitness, creative agencies, bold brands):
- Display: Syne ExtraBold, Space Grotesk Bold, Clash Display Bold
- Body: Manrope, Outfit, Schibsted Grotesk
- Feel: Energetic, contemporary, forward-thinking

**Never use:** more than 2 typeface families, system fonts (Helvetica/Arial) for headlines, script fonts for body or UI text.

## COPY TONE

Derive from the brief's audience and personality. Default principles:

**Do:**
- Lead with the client's situation or desire: what brings them here, what they want
- State the outcome: what their life/situation looks like after working with this business
- Be specific — generic claims ("best in town," "passionate about service") convert nobody
- Match the energy of the brand — calm brands use calm copy, bold brands use bold copy
- Make the next step obvious and frictionless

**Don't:**
- Lead with the company's history before establishing what the visitor gets
- Use industry jargon the client's customers wouldn't use
- Make the copy about the business rather than the customer
- Use vague CTAs ("learn more," "click here," "explore")

## GENERAL TRUST SIGNALS

Apply in priority order based on what the brief provides:

1. **Real reviews / star rating** — Google, Yelp, or platform-specific
2. **Years in business** — longevity = reliability
3. **Clients / customers served** — specific numbers
4. **Named team / faces** — real people build trust
5. **Before/after or results** — transformation evidence
6. **Awards or recognition** — local, industry, or platform
7. **Press or features** — "as seen in"
8. **Certifications or credentials** — relevant training, licenses, memberships

## COMMON OBJECTIONS (General — adapt per business type)

| Objection | Where to address |
|---|---|
| "Is this worth the price?" | Near pricing, testimonials, results section |
| "Are they any good?" | Reviews, portfolio, credentials |
| "Can I trust them?" | Team photos, real names, reviews, years in business |
| "Is this right for me?" | Services detail, FAQ |
| "How does it work?" | Process steps, FAQ |
| "How do I get started?" | CTA, contact, process steps |

## NAV STRUCTURE (General Default)

```
[Logo]    Services/Menu ▾    About    [Location if applicable]    Contact    [Primary CTA Button]
```

Adapt dropdown labels to the business type:
- Restaurant: Menu, Reservations, About, Contact
- Fitness studio: Classes, Membership, About, Contact, Schedule
- Beauty salon: Services, Gallery, About, Book Now
- Photography: Portfolio, Services, About, Contact
- Coaching: Programs, About, Testimonials, Contact

## HOMEPAGE SECTION ORDER (General Default)

**Recommended starting order — not mandatory.** Reorder, merge, or drop sections when
this business gives you a reason; state the reason in one line in the Compliance Report.
Sections for pages or services this client doesn't have are simply omitted, never
padded with filler.


Adapt based on the brief's conversion goal and business type:

1. Announcement bar (key offer + phone/booking link — if applicable)
2. Hero (outcome-led headline, dual CTA, trust micro-element)
3. Trust/Stats bar (reviews, years, clients — whatever exists)
4. Brand story / positioning panel
5. Services or offerings preview
6. Portfolio / gallery (if visual business)
7. Process / how it works (if applicable)
8. Testimonials / reviews
9. Team (if people-forward business)
10. Locations (if applicable)
11. CTA conversion band
12. FAQ + contact/booking form (PageOutro)
13. Footer

**Adapt this order** to the conversion goal. A restaurant leads with the menu and reservation. A fitness studio leads with the class schedule and free trial. A photographer leads with the portfolio.

## PHOTOGRAPHY DIRECTION (General)

- Real, human, authentic — never generic stock clichés
- Lifestyle photography preferred over product-only shots
- Consistent editing style across all images (temperature, contrast, saturation)
- For food/hospitality: warm, appetizing, ambient light
- For fitness/wellness: energy, movement, real bodies — not model-perfect
- For creative services: the work itself is the hero — portfolio shots front and center
- For coaching/education: genuine human connection, not staged boardroom shots
- Never: fake smiling people in suits, generic handshakes, cliché stock
