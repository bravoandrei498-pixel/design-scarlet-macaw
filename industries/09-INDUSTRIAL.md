# Design Scarlet Macaw — Industrial Industry Playbook
### Typical fits (not exhaustive): Manufacturing, Construction, Engineering, Trades
### (Electrical, Plumbing, HVAC), Landscaping — and any other project- or job-site
### business (roofing, paving, fabrication, logistics, equipment hire, facilities, solar).


## HOW TO READ THIS PLAYBOOK

**This file narrows the search space. It does not choose the design.**

1. **Client brand assets always win.** If the brief supplies real brand colors, fonts,
   or a logo, those are the anchor — build around them, never instead of them.
   Everything below is the fallback for a business with no established brand.
2. **Constraints bind; recommendations don't.** "Never / Avoid" items are real — they
   exist because those choices reliably misfire in this vertical. Palette leans, type
   archetypes, component lists, and section orders are **starting points and menus**:
   take what this specific business justifies, leave the rest.
3. **Never ship a whole list.** Every list here is a menu, not a checklist. Taking all
   of it is precisely how ten different businesses end up with the same website.
4. **Trends move; this file will lag them.** Where a current, better-executed pattern
   serves the same goal, use it and note the swap in one line in the Compliance Report.
   The target is a site that looks made in the year it shipped — not one that matches
   this document.

justifies — services actually offered, pages actually checked, assets that actually
exist. A typical build uses **3–5** of them. If the brief gives you no reason to
include one, leave it out: a thin section is worse than an absent one.

## PALETTE GUIDANCE

*Starting directions, not a prescription. If the client has brand colors, those win.
The 3 build directions must not all sit in the same corner of this space (Rule 1).*

Industrial sites must project strength, reliability, and competence — not corporate sterility.

**Construction / general contracting:**
- Deep charcoal, slate, or dark navy as anchor
- Warm orange, amber, or construction yellow as accent (authentic to the trade, energetic)
- Concrete gray, warm white, or off-white for backgrounds
- Feel: Strong, capable, trustworthy

**Manufacturing / engineering:**
- Deep navy or charcoal + steel blue or slate
- Silver/metallic accent or industrial green
- Clean white backgrounds
- Feel: Precise, reliable, professional

**Trades (electrical, plumbing, HVAC):**
- Can be bolder and more direct than manufacturing
- Deep navy or charcoal + strong accent (orange, red, yellow — trade colors are accepted here)
- White + neutral backgrounds
- Feel: Fast, reliable, local, expert

**Landscaping / outdoor services:**
- Deep green or forest green as primary
- Warm earth tones (terracotta, sand, warm brown) as secondary
- Off-white or cream backgrounds
- Feel: Craft, nature, transformation

**Note:** Red is acceptable as an accent for industrial builds — it reads as urgency/action in this context (emergency trades, fire protection, etc.) rather than danger.

## TYPOGRAPHY GUIDANCE

**Recommended archetypes:**

**Industrial Powerhouse** (construction, trades, manufacturing):
- Display: Condensed heavy sans (Barlow Condensed ExtraBold, Bebas Neue, Anton)
- Body: Clean sans (Barlow Regular, Work Sans, Public Sans)
- Feel: Strong, no-nonsense, reliable, built to last

**Premium Professional** (engineering, specialized manufacturing, high-end contractors):
- Display: Confident sans (DM Sans ExtraBold, Satoshi Black)
- Body: Geist, Work Sans
- Feel: Precise, professional, premium

**Never use:**
- Serif display fonts (reads too refined/soft for most industrial)
- Script or decorative fonts
- Thin or light-weight display fonts (projects fragility, wrong for trades)

## COPY TONE

Industrial copy must be direct, confident, and specific. No fluff.

**Do:**
- Lead with capability and track record: "25 years building [City]'s commercial infrastructure"
- Be specific about what you do and the scale: "Electrical contractor for commercial, industrial, and residential projects from 500 sq ft to 500,000 sq ft"
- Highlight reliability and dependability: "On time. On budget. Every project."
- Use project-specific language: square footage, project types, client types served
- Let the work speak: portfolio and case studies are the primary sales tool

**Don't:**
- Use vague marketing language ("passionate about excellence," "committed to quality") without specifics
- Over-explain — industrial clients are decision-makers who want facts fast
- Use soft, emotional language that doesn't match the industry tone
- Bury the service area — local trades clients want to know you're nearby

## INDUSTRIAL-SPECIFIC TRUST SIGNALS

Priority order:

1. **Years in business** — longevity = stability in this industry
2. **Projects completed / scale** — "500+ projects," "$50M+ in completed work"
3. **Licensed, bonded, insured** — visible and prominent (reduces risk perception immediately)
4. **Certifications** — OSHA, ISO, contractor license number, trade-specific certifications
5. **Notable project portfolio** — real photos of real work
6. **Client testimonials** — from recognizable local businesses or project types
7. **Team size / capacity** — signals ability to handle the scope of work
8. **Service area** — local credibility and proximity

## HOMEPAGE SECTION ORDER (Industrial)

**Recommended starting order — not mandatory.** Reorder, merge, or drop sections when
this business gives you a reason; state the reason in one line in the Compliance Report.
Sections for pages or services this client doesn't have are simply omitted, never
padded with filler.


1. Announcement bar (license number / "licensed, bonded, insured" + phone)
2. Hero (capability headline, dual CTA, trust micro-element — years in business or projects completed)
3. Trust/Stats bar (years in business, projects completed, cities served, certifications)
4. Brand story / company positioning (founding story, values, what makes them reliable)
5. Services preview grid (photo of work, not icons)
6. Project portfolio / featured work (3–4 featured projects)
7. Process / How We Work (step cards)
8. Testimonials (real client quotes with company/project context)
9. Certifications / licenses section
10. Service area (map or city list)
11. CTA conversion band ("Get a Quote" / "Let's Talk About Your Project")
12. FAQ + Quote request form (PageOutro)
13. Footer

## PROJECT PORTFOLIO
*(Industrial-specific section)*

Portfolio is the primary conversion tool for industrial clients. Treat it as a showcase, not a gallery.

**Structure:**
- 3–6 featured projects on homepage (full-bleed or card style)
- Each project: hero photo + project name + type + location + key scope (size, value if appropriate)
- "View All Projects" to full portfolio page
- Full portfolio page: filterable by project type (commercial, residential, industrial, etc.)
- Case study format for marquee projects: challenge → approach → outcome + photos

## PHOTOGRAPHY DIRECTION

- **Authentic job site and finished project photography is the product**
- During construction: workers in action, equipment operating, progress shots
- Finished work: architectural quality, wide-angle, well-lit
- Team/crew: on-site in work gear — never staged in a studio
- Equipment and fleet: if it's a selling point, showcase it
- Never: generic stock (hard hat on desk, handshake on a blueprint)
- Consistent editing: industrial sites can have a slightly bolder, higher-contrast treatment than healthcare or legal
- Before/after is extremely effective for renovation, landscaping, and fit-out projects
