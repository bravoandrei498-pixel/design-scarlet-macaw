# Design Scarlet Macaw — Legal Industry Playbook
### Typical fits (not exhaustive): Personal Injury, Family Law, Criminal Defense, Business
### Law, Estate Planning, Immigration — and any other credential-gated advisory practice
### (accounting, tax, financial advisory, insurance broking, notary, IP/patent, consulting).


## HOW TO READ THIS PLAYBOOK

**This file narrows the search space. It does not choose the design.**

1. **Client brand assets always win.** If the brief supplies real brand colors, fonts,
   or a logo, those are the anchor — build around them, never instead of them.
   Everything below is the fallback for a business with no established brand.
2. **Constraints bind; recommendations don't.** "Never / Avoid" items are real — they
   exist because those choices reliably misfire in this vertical. Palette leans, type
   archetypes, component lists, and section orders are **starting points and menus**:
   take what this specific business justifies, leave the rest.
3. **Never ship a whole list.** Every list here is a menu, not a checklist. Taking all
   of it is precisely how ten different businesses end up with the same website.
4. **Trends move; this file will lag them.** Where a current, better-executed pattern
   serves the same goal, use it and note the swap in one line in the Compliance Report.
   The target is a site that looks made in the year it shipped — not one that matches
   this document.

justifies — services actually offered, pages actually checked, assets that actually
exist. A typical build uses **3–5** of them. If the brief gives you no reason to
include one, leave it out: a thin section is worse than an absent one.

## PALETTE GUIDANCE

*Starting directions, not a prescription. If the client has brand colors, those win.
The 3 build directions must not all sit in the same corner of this space (Rule 1).*

Legal sites must convey authority, credibility, and trustworthiness — but not cold bureaucracy.

**Avoid:**
- Overly bright or playful colors (undermines authority)
- Pure black + white only (reads as generic, low-budget)
- Aggressive red (reads as ambulance-chaser, not trusted counsel)

**Lean toward:**
- Deep navy, charcoal, or dark slate as primary dark anchor
- Warm cream, off-white, or warm gray as background neutrals
- Gold, amber, or warm bronze as the accent (signals premium, established)
- For family law / estate planning: can introduce warmth (terracotta, sage) without losing authority
- For criminal defense: bolder, more confident — deep navy + sharp contrast
- For business law: more corporate — navy + slate + gold

**One cohesive palette — never more than 3 brand colors + neutrals.**

## TYPOGRAPHY GUIDANCE

**Recommended archetypes:**

**Warm Authority** (family law, estate planning, immigration):
- Display: Elegant serif (Playfair Display, Cormorant Garamond, Libre Baskerville)
- Body: Clean humanist sans (Public Sans, Source Sans 3)
- Feel: Trusted, established, wise

**Premium Professional** (personal injury, business law):
- Display: Humanist display sans (DM Sans ExtraBold, Satoshi, Plus Jakarta Sans)
- Body: Instrument Sans, DM Sans
- Feel: Modern, confident, results-driven

**Bold Modern** (criminal defense, younger firms):
- Display: Grotesk heavy (Syne ExtraBold, Space Grotesk Bold)
- Body: Schibsted Grotesk, Outfit
- Feel: Fearless, sharp, contemporary

**Never use:**
- Script or decorative fonts (undermines authority)
- Condensed industrial fonts (wrong for legal unless used very selectively)

## COPY TONE

Legal copy must project authority while making the prospective client feel heard and supported.

**Do:**
- Lead with the client's situation: "Facing a personal injury claim alone is overwhelming."
- State the outcome: "We fight to get you the compensation you deserve."
- Use specific language about results: verdicts, settlements, cases won (where ethically permitted)
- Make the process feel clear and approachable — reduce "legal fear"
- Use "we" language that positions the firm as on the client's side

**Don't:**
- Use excessive legal jargon in client-facing copy
- Lead with the firm's history before establishing empathy
- Make guarantees about outcomes (ethically prohibited)
- Use aggressive "call now" urgency language that feels predatory
- Use generic copy: "experienced attorneys," "dedicated to justice" — be specific

**Attorney disclaimer:** Required on contact forms and case evaluation forms. "Contacting us does not create an attorney-client relationship."

## LEGAL-SPECIFIC TRUST SIGNALS

Priority order:

1. **Case results / verdicts** — specific dollar amounts where permitted ("$2.3M settlement")
2. **Years of experience** — combined experience of the firm
3. **Cases won / clients served** — specific numbers
4. **Named attorneys with credentials** — bar admissions, law school, specializations
5. **Industry recognitions** — Super Lawyers, Martindale-Hubbell AV Rating, Best Lawyers
6. **Client testimonials** — real names, real outcomes, real gratitude
7. **Free consultation** — reduces the barrier to first contact significantly
8. **No fee unless we win** (personal injury) — eliminates the financial objection

## COMMON CLIENT OBJECTIONS (Address in design)

| Objection | Where to address |
|---|---|
| "I can't afford a lawyer" | Hero area ("no fee unless we win" / free consult) |
| "My case isn't worth pursuing" | Services/practice area copy, FAQ |
| "How long will this take?" | FAQ, process steps |
| "What are my chances?" | FAQ, results section (ethically framed) |
| "Is this firm right for my case?" | Practice areas page, attorney bios |
| "I don't know where to start" | First consultation section, clear process steps |
| "Will I have to go to court?" | FAQ, practice area detail copy |

## HOMEPAGE SECTION ORDER (Legal)

**Recommended starting order — not mandatory.** Reorder, merge, or drop sections when
this business gives you a reason; state the reason in one line in the Compliance Report.
Sections for pages or services this client doesn't have are simply omitted, never
padded with filler.


1. Announcement bar (free consultation + phone)
2. Hero (client situation headline, dual CTA, trust micro-element — results or rating)
3. Trust/Stats bar (cases won, years experience, clients helped, recognition)
4. Brand story / Firm positioning
5. Practice areas preview grid
6. Case results / verdicts (where permitted)
7. Meet the Attorneys
8. Testimonials (real client quotes with attribution)
9. Process / What to Expect
10. Why Choose Us
11. CTA conversion band ("Get the representation you deserve")
12. FAQ + Free consultation form (PageOutro)
13. Footer

## COMPLIANCE REQUIREMENTS

- **Attorney-client disclaimer** below every case evaluation / contact form
- Privacy Policy linked in footer
- Bar admission information on attorney bios
- No guarantee of specific outcomes in copy
- Results must be noted as past results not guaranteeing future outcomes (where required by state bar)

## PHOTOGRAPHY DIRECTION

- Confident, professional attorney shots — candid in office setting, not stiff courtroom poses
- Real office environment preferred over generic stock
- Client-facing: warm, approachable, human — not intimidating
- For PI: can include courthouse exteriors, negotiation table settings
- Never: staged handshakes, gavel on desk, Lady Justice statue stock photos (extremely cliché)
- Consistent professional editing across all shots
